# Grasp Execution Without a Planner :Configuration-Space Grasp Distance Fields with Certified Safety & Guaranteed Quality 

Clinton Enwerem 1, John S. Baras 1, and Calin Belta 1

Abstract —Standard multifingered grasp execution architectures plan a collision-free trajectory to a selected grasp pose and track it with a feedback law. Execution-time object pose uncertainty or perturbations may invalidate the planned trajectory, forcing a costly replanning step. We present Grasp Distance Fields (GDFs), smooth softmin distance fields over grasp configurations in the arm-hand configuration space. Our controller executes a grasp by following the negative GDF gradient with a stationary feedback law, eliminating the need for a planner, stored trajectory, or grasp selection. For safety, we filter the resulting command through a CBF-CLF quadratic program (QP), constraining self-collision, workspace, object, and obstacle clearance, reporting impeded progress as explicit slack. We prove that the softmin tracks the true set distance within log N/ρ for N candidates and smoothing parameter ρ, and that the filtered closed loop renders the safe set forward invariant. Since no smooth field captures hand-object contact switching, we switch between hand closure and grasp retention modes using hysteresis at a pregrasp configuration, trading object collision avoidance for contact admission. A wrench-quality CBF then bounds the realized grasp’s force-closure margin within a prescribed tolerance of its value at hold onset. We evaluate in kinematic simulation on a fixed-base arm and a Unitree G1 humanoid, both fitted with the same underactuated hand. Our controller navigates cluttered and dynamic scenes to safely reach for, grasp, and lift 46 of 50 test objects spanning primitive, household, and adversarial classes. Across the 46 lifts, the executed grasps retain a median 94% of their synthesized quality margin, at 0.09 ms QP solve time per 20 ms control step. Project page: www.clintonenwerem.com/gdf .

Index Terms —Safety-critical control, dexterous grasping, con-trol barrier functions, reactive execution, grasp quality metrics. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The execution stage of multifingered grasping remains dominated by a plan-then-track architecture. A grasp generator proposes a set of candidate grasps [ 1], [ 2 ], a selection step produces a single grasp from this set, and a motion planner generates a collision-free trajectory to the selected pose, which a tracking controller then follows through approach, hand closure, and object relocation [ 3]. However, under execution-time object pose uncertainty or output perturbations, the planned trajectory may become invalid, forcing a replanning step. Replanning is computationally expensive and adds latency to the system’s reaction time, motivating closed-loop and reactive grasping [ 4 ], [5]. By contrast, we omit the replanning step entirely and repre-sent the grasp execution target as a set of grasp configurations. We then define a smooth distance field over this set in the configuration space of the coupled arm-hand system, and specify a stationary feedback law on the resulting field. As such,

A. Related Work 

Operational-space potential field methods place an attractive potential at the goal and repulsive potentials on obstacles, evaluated at each control step [ 9]. Their failure mode, a local minimum away from the goal, has motivated modifications since. The CBF formulation retains the reactive character while converting obstacle avoidance from a penalty into a arXiv:2608.00600v2 [cs.RO] 4 Aug 2026 2

constraint with a forward-invariance guarantee. Every potential field induces a barrier function while the converse fails, and the CBF therefore strictly generalizes the potential field [ 10 ], [7]. Beyond reactive navigation, CBF methods extend to grasping and manipulation. The authors of [ 11 ] apply them to grasp tracking through controllers that certify no slip and no roll-off, and [ 12 ] enforces contact-force and closure constraints from measured contacts during the grasp itself. On robotic manipulators, the same filters operate at every control step in clutter, intervening around dense dynamic obstacles in a cooking cell [ 5]. Perception-built safety functions omit the hand-designed distance model entirely, solving a Poisson problem on occupancy data so that the CBF value comes from measured geometry [ 13 ], [ 14 ], with predictive humanoid implementations showing the construction at whole-body scale [ 15 ]. Since a multifingered grasp is not a point, extending such field-based methods to grasping requires the definition of a grasp target. By contrast, grasp fields provide the missing target without addressing execution. Neural grasp distance fields learn the distance from a query pose to the manifold of valid grasps for an object, interpret it as a cost, and minimize it inside a trajectory optimizer jointly with smoothness and collision terms [ 16 ]. Such fields are defined in task space, which requires solving inverse kinematics at execution time. Configuration-space distance fields resolve this for obstacle geometry, demonstrating that a distance representation in joint space admits one-step gradient projection where task-space signed distance fields fail on the nonlinearity of the kinematics [ 17 ]. Our field extends this construction from obstacle boundaries to a discrete set of grasp configurations, and our controller evaluates the field at every control step, whereas the prior work embeds it in an optimizer. Beyond grasp-target fields, candidate grasp sets are widely available. Grasp generators produce grasp poses or full hand configurations for grippers and dexterous hands alike, through analytical [ 3], [ 18 ] and generative [ 1 ], [ 2 ], [ 19 ] approaches. The generated grasps satisfy some desired quality metric, from deterministic force closure [ 20 ], [ 21 ] to robust quality measures [ 22 ], [ 18 ], [ 6] that define analytic bounds on the probability of force closure under uncertain friction and object normals. Given a candidate grasp set, our controller operates directly on the individual grasps through a tuple of the object-relative hand-root pose and the corresponding hand configuration (a grasp descriptor , Definition III.1). Execution software plans reaches to a selected pose, closed-loop grasping appears as a learned capability inside individual systems [ 4], and end-to-end policies embed execution in their network weights [ 23 ], [ 24 ]. None of the three exposes a grasp-set target a reactive controller evaluates pointwise, which our grasp distance fields supply. 

B. Contributions 

Specifically, we make five contributions. i. Grasp Distance Field and Smooth Approximation : we introduce the notion of a grasp distance field defined over a discrete grasp candidate set in configuration space and establish its two-sided distance bound, smoothness, and bounded-gradient properties. The softmax weights of the resulting field encode the candidate selection as a continuous combination, which eliminates the discrete selection step (Section IV). ii. A Certified Safe Execution Law : we give a nominal control along the field’s negative gradient, filtered through a combined control Lyapunov and control barrier function quadratic program whose barrier constraints cover self-collision, workspace, object, and obstacle clearance, whose Lyapunov constraints record impeded progress toward the grasp as explicit slack, and whose equality block encodes the dependent-joint constraints of an underactuated hand. We prove that the resulting closed loop renders the safe set forward invariant, and our wrench-quality constraint holds the risk-adjusted force-closure certificate of [ 6] within a prescribed tolerance from hold onset (Section V). iii. A Contact-Mode Switch with Hysteresis : we give the mode structure that takes execution through contact, the single event a smooth field cannot express, stated as a hybrid system with per-mode constraint sets (Section V-B). iv. A Wrench-Certificate Bridge : we restrict admission to grasps whose risk-adjusted force-closure margin [ 6 ] ex-ceeds a threshold and evaluate the executed grasp under the same margin, connecting the joint-space field to the wrench-space certificates of the grasping literature and re-porting the min-weight margin as a baseline (Section V-C ). v. Full-Model Verification : we verify the controller numer-ically on a 7-degree-of-freedom arm with an 11-joint multifingered hand, including a 50-object grasp descriptor evaluation, a moving-obstacle case on the same constraints, and a humanoid G1 instantiation that runs the controller without modification. In a recorded execution over a five-candidate set, the per-step softmax weights concentrate on the nearest candidate at every step, with no separate selection step in the loop (Section VI).

The minimum value of the obstacle constraint is 3.8 mm with 3.4 cm of true surface clearance, the filtered palm path lies up to 9.7 cm from the nominal path, and the torso rotation stays under 18 ◦ through the lift. However, the same contact gap we quantify in Section VI-B appears at hold onset. The guard is satisfied with three fingers in contact before the thumb arrives, and both the risk-adjusted margin and the min-weight baseline place the executed three-contact set below closure, against a descriptor whose four-contact closure has ε(β) = 4 .31 × 10 −3 and a min-weight margin of 0.48 .

F. Verification

The analytic gradient (9) matches central finite differences to a maximum error of 7.7 × 10 −10 , which is the finite-difference noise floor at a step of 10 −6. The gradient bound of Proposi-tion IV.1 holds at a maximum Λ−1-norm of 1 + 2 .2 × 10 −16 ,unity to one unit in the last place. Similarly, the bound (8) holds on both sides, with no violation on the lower side and an upper gap of at most 

3.12 × 10 −2 against the ceiling log 5 /ρ = 6 .44 × 10 −2. The 200 sampled configurations therefore exercise the bound where it is nearly tight, at the Voronoi boundaries between distinct descriptors. by the end of the trials, as (13) predicts. VII. D ISCUSSION 

The closed-loop trajectory of our CBF quadratic program is a collision-free curve in Q, ending at contact admission where a conventional plan-then-track system completes its planned reach and begins closure. We add our wrench-quality CBF at hold onset, and Proposition V.1 bounds the margin’s decay. Barrier constraints tuned for slip and roll-off [ 11 ], or risk-sensitive constraints over latent uncertainty [ 44 ], enter (11) through the same hard-constraint block and leave Theorem V.1 unchanged. 

A. Trapped Equilibria of the Filtered Closed Loop 

Our construction inherits the classical limitation of artificial potential fields. The field (7) has an undesired local minimum whenever the constraints of (11) eliminate every direction of decrease away from the candidate set and leave the projected gradient with no tangential component, the same failure potential fields exhibit in nonconvex clutter [ 9], and the blocked-approach scene of Fig. 2(c) pictures the resulting stop. The set-valued target mitigates this failure without eliminating it. Many candidates enlarge the region of attraction, and the admission test eliminates the candidates an obstacle blocks. When no remaining candidate admits a collision-free path, neither the larger candidate set nor the admission test creates such a path. Indeed, disabling admission on the five-candidate set of Section VI-F leaves all five candidates in the target set and traps the closed-loop trajectory at a field value of 0.144 , just above the 0.12 guard threshold, with the joint velocity decaying to 

10 −14 . With the admission test in place, the blocked candidate drops out and the controller enters CLOSE at step 82. Admission is a static test of the pregrasp and closure configurations, and its residual gap shows with the box obstacle next to the approach path, where four of five candidates satisfy admission yet the controller still stops in CLOSE without a third contact, neither completing the grasp nor contacting an obstacle. We map the boundary of the trapped region with a scene-placement search. A 50 cm column square on the approach path leaves the closed loop trapped in every trial, the controller never completes when the overlap between the obstacle and the region the palm passes through exceeds 7 cm , always completes at 3 cm , and the boundary moves with about 2 mm of obstacle placement. In the reported scene, the trajectory passes around a first obstacle before reaching the obstacle on its line, and avoids the trapped region. B. Limitations 

Our field depends on candidates synthesized for the current object pose, and a moving object requires the candidate generator to update G at every perception step. On hardware a pose estimate replaces ground truth, where measurement-robust constraints incorporate bounded state error into the CBF margin at a known cost in conservatism [ 45 ]. The verification reported here is kinematic, the executed grasps hold a quasi-static closure certificate while the lift moves an attached object, and we do not test on hardware. The tactile contact source of Section V-B defines the interface such trials would use, filters of the same form already operate at every control step on dynamic legged platforms [ 46 ], [ 15 ], [ 47 ], and the open question under dynamics concerns the wrench certificate alone. VIII. C ONCLUSION 

We introduced grasp distance fields and a certified execution law for safe multifingered grasping. Our softmin distance field over the pregrasp configurations provides a region of attraction in place of discrete grasp selection, and our CBF quadratic program enforces safety at every control step. A hysteresis-based mode structure admits contact without a replanning step, and our wrench-quality constraint holds the risk-adjusted force-closure margin within a prescribed tolerance through the lift. On an 18-degree-of-freedom arm-hand system, the full control step costs 2 ms against the 20 ms control period, and the same controller runs on a Unitree G1 humanoid arm-hand chain without modification.
